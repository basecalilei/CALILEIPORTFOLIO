# Texwax Identity

Covers: naming · visual identity & art direction · voice & tone.
This is the least-built area of the company — and per the founder's own claim ("they wouldn't win against me in branding and marketing"), the one where the moat is supposed to live. Treat identity decisions as structural, not cosmetic.

---

## 1. Naming — company `SET`, system `OPEN`

### Company — `SET` (D2)
- **Texwax.** Neutral, ownable, no heritage baggage.
- **"Patina Wax" is retired** as a name. It was a placeholder for the living-surface thesis, which was demoted from hook to depth layer. It's also descriptive/generic (existing term in furniture/leather care → trademark and search problems).
- The word *patina* survives in the brand vocabulary as the depth story — never as the headline.

### Naming system — `OPEN` (OPEN-5)
The real design problem: the system must name **2 anchors + an endless stream of drops** without exhausting itself or collapsing into randomness. Requirements gathered so far:
- Scales indefinitely (drops are perpetual).
- Distinguishes anchors (permanent, storefront) from drops (limited, collectible) — possibly different naming registers.
- Names must work as reel titles / on-screen text (short, punchy, sayable).
- Should carry the transformation/fun register, not heritage-reverent tones.
- Graduation wrinkle: a drop's name follows it when it becomes an anchor — so drop names can't be throwaway jokes that would embarrass a permanent SKU.
Candidate scheme *types* to explore in session (not proposals yet): paint-industry-style evocative color names; numbered formula codes (lab register); place/reference names; effect-descriptive names. Decide the register first, then generate.

## 2. Visual identity & art direction — `OPEN` (OPEN-6)

Nothing designed yet. Raw material on record:

- **Founder taste datapoint #1 (strongest signal):** his favorite formula is the **white wax**, which on tan boots produces "the moon-dusted patina of Apollo-era gear." The world inside that image: program-era industrial, matte, dusty, functional-worn, white-on-worn, NASA-adjacent utility. This is data about the founder's eye — not yet a chosen direction.
- **Tension to resolve deliberately:** the Apollo/industrial instinct vs. the "fun, impulse, playful transformation" positioning. They can be fused (utilitarian-playful is a real lane) but shouldn't be blended accidentally.
- Surfaces to design: logo/wordmark, palette (careful: the *product* is a color system — the brand palette must frame, not compete), the padded zip mailer, stickers, quick-start card, bar labeling/sealing, and above all **the Instagram feed** — the primary brand surface customers actually see.
- The cinematic reel aesthetic ("staged, vibey") and the visual identity must be designed together; the feed is where identity lives.

## 3. Voice & tone — `OPEN` (OPEN-7)

- **Rejected:** the heritage-reverent register (provenance, tradition, ritual care). That was the old Patina Wax voice and the old audience.
- **Direction (undefined):** fun, confident, transformation-forward. Talks to a fashion impulse buyer, not a gear-care hobbyist.
- **Logged voice data, not canon:** the founder's old product writing had a distinctive declarative, aphoristic register — honest about failure modes ("waxed garments aren't delicate — they just need respect. You maintain them like tools."). The *register* (plainspoken, declarative, no coddling) may survive even though the heritage content it carried does not. Test this in session.
- **Honesty is a voice requirement, not just a content rule:** the brand tells buyers the easy method works, what cure time really is, what care really involves. Plainspoken honesty is on-brand; hype that overpromises is off-brand and generates the one-star reviews the impulse segment is prone to.
- To write: voice principles, vocabulary (words we use / never use), caption conventions, customer-service tone (troubleshooting scripts in texwaxProduct should be rewritten in final voice once defined).
