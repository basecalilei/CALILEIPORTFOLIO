## 1. Positioning & wedge — `SET`

### The category default
Almost everyone in fabric wax sells one product: a single **clear** wax (typically beeswax + paraffin + oils) producing a glossy finish with a slightly tacky hand-feel. One look, one feel — the waxed-canvas / waxed-jacket aesthetic. And the entire category frames wax the same way: **maintenance**. Care for the gear you already own and baby. Reproof your Barbour. Protect your Filson.

### The Texwax thesis
**Wax is a medium for transforming surfaces, not maintaining them.**

Texwax sells cheap, fun, low-commitment transformation of things you don't wash — a $25 bar that recolors and re-textures boots, shoes, hats, and bags. Different verb (transform, not protect), different customer (fashion impulse buyer, not heritage maintainer), different shelf.

### Why accessories-first (the maintenance wall)
Learned from the founder's year of wearing his own waxed clothing:
- Waxed garments are wash-hostile. You don't want to machine-wash (strips the wax), hand-washing is a chore, even the careful method (inside-out, net bag, cold) feels like a hassle.
- Result: for anyone with a full closet, the waxed garment just hangs there, forgotten. The founder abandoned his own.
- Waxed clothing therefore self-selects for a rare customer: the person with few clothes and one beloved jacket they're happy to "maintain." Small population, wrong target.
- **Accessories dodge the wall entirely.** You don't wash boots, hats, or bags. Transform once, enjoy for months or years.

### Why boots specifically (the beachhead)
- Combat/work boot makers ship black, brown, tan, green. That's it.
- Dyeing boots barely works: soles won't take dye, and mixed materials dye unevenly.
- Wax **recolors** boots — even the soles hold a surface coat (sits on top rather than absorbing).
- The first coat lands slightly uneven, which reads as an attractive weathered look immediately.
- Patina then develops over time as a bonus — depth the customer discovers, not a promise they're sold.
- Durability: one good coat ≈ 6 months at 5×/week wear; ≈ 2 years for a light-rotation fashion consumer. This is transform-once territory, not a maintenance treadmill.

### The range as engine
The founder's wax matrix supports an effectively unlimited space of colors/finishes (see texwaxProduct). Strategically the range is three things at once:
1. **Differentiation** — everyone else has one clear wax; Texwax has a palette.
2. **The repeat engine** — customers won't return to rewax (they forget), but they will return for new colors and new items to transform. Collection behavior, not maintenance behavior.
3. **The content engine** — every new formula is a new video (see drop model, texwaxCommercial).

### The acknowledged trade (record of a real disagreement)
Leading with "fun cheap recolor" instead of the deeper patina/living-surface worldview buys **reach** (zero education needed, travels on reels) at the cost of **defensibility** (anyone can pour tinted wax and film a before/after; a worldview is harder to clone). This trade was made consciously, given a bootstrap budget that can't fund market education. Mitigation: patina is held in reserve as the depth layer — the thing that surfaces in month three and makes Texwax more than a novelty. **Hook shallow; keep depth in reserve.**

## 2. Target customer — `PARTIAL`

### Primary (working profile)
The **impulse transformer**: a fashion/shoe consumer who sees a cinematic before/after reel, thinks "recoloring my boots would be neat," and buys at $25 because the price makes it a fun experiment rather than a commitment. Owns multiple pairs of shoes; cares about look, not gear-care ritual.

### Anti-customer (explicit)
The **heritage maintainer**: loves the living-surface philosophy, rewaxes one denim jacket for a decade. Founder's assessment: "few and far between." They may purchase anyway — great — but nothing is designed for them, and their language (provenance, tradition, reverence) is not the brand's language.

### Known risk in the primary target
Impulse buyers are the highest-disappointment segment: low intent + an expectation gap (hero reels show expert brush/melt results; buyers will mostly rub a bar on). Managed by decisions D6/D9 (see texwaxDecisions): the honesty rule in content and a quick-start that makes the easy path legitimate.

### Logged hypothesis (unvalidated)
**Raw denim / fade culture** is the psychological adjacent community — people who run fade competitions, photograph creases, treat aging as achievement. Texwax generalizes that psychology to any surface. Potentially a seeding channel; do not build the brand around it (they skew heritage).

### To do (OPEN-8)
Sharpen into a real profile: age range, style tribes (workwear-fashion, techwear, streetwear, DIY/customization culture — e.g. the sneaker-customizing world), where they actually are on Instagram, what accounts they follow, what makes them share.

## 3. Brand foundation — `OPEN`

Deliberately not faked. What exists as raw material:

- **The core verb:** transform. What we stand against: the idea that your stuff has to stay the color it was sold in.
- **Founder taste data:** the white wax is his favorite — on tan boots it produces "the moon-dusted patina of Apollo-era gear." Program-era industrial, matte, dusty, worn-functional. First hard datapoint of the brand's aesthetic instincts.
- **The demoted-but-alive depth story:** patina, surfaces that record use, "no two age the same." True, differentiating, and reserved for retention depth rather than acquisition hook.
- **Register note:** the founder's old product writing was declarative and aphoristic ("you maintain them like tools"). Distinctive; logged as voice data, not canon (see texwaxIdentity).

To write in a dedicated session: mission, core belief, why Texwax exists, what it refuses to be. Must be consistent with the wedge — playful transformation first, depth underneath.

## 4. Competitive landscape — `OPEN`

Framing to research against: **they sell care; we sell change.**

Known players to map (not yet researched properly):
- **Otter Wax** — the default DTC fabric-wax brand; likely the closest shelf-neighbor.
- **Barbour** — sells reproofing wax as maintenance for its own jackets; defines the heritage register.
- **Filson** — same pattern; oil-finish wax for its own goods.
- **Fjällräven Greenland Wax** — mass-adjacent, sold as garment care.
- Generic beeswax/canvas wax products (Amazon/Etsy tier) — the low end.
- **Adjacent, possibly more important than wax competitors:** shoe/sneaker customization culture (Angelus paints, custom-sneaker artists) — they own "transform your shoes" today, with paint. Texwax's real positioning fight may be against paint, not against other waxes: wax vs. paint = texture/patina/reversibility vs. hard permanent coating. Research this.

Research tasks: price points, formats (tin vs bar), how each talks (verbs, imagery), where they show up on Instagram, what their customers complain about (reviews = free market research), whether anyone sells colored fabric wax at all.
