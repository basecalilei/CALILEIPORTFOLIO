# Texwax Decision Log

The spine of the company documentation. Every resolved decision lives here with its date, reasoning, and what was rejected. Decisions are cross-cutting — a price is commercial, a content rule is marketing — so the log sits above the category files, and the category files inherit from it.

**Rules for maintaining this log:**
- New decisions get logged the session they're made: date, decision, reasoning, rejected alternatives.
- When a decision closes an OPEN item, move it out of the OPEN list.
- SET decisions are not re-litigated unless the founder explicitly reopens them. If reopened, log the reversal — don't silently edit history.

## SET — decided

### D1 · Company model: DTC consumer brand (2026-07-17)
**Decision:** Texwax launches as a direct-to-consumer brand selling bars of wax online, acquiring customers through Instagram (organic reels + paid ads).
**Reasoning:** Matches the founder's actual position — bootstrapped, time-rich, strong content-production skills. Chosen by founder instinct when presented with the four-way fork.
**Rejected (for launch, not forever):** B2B finish supplier to apparel brands; premium heritage-adjacent maker brand; abstract "finish system" play (swatch-deck positioning). B2B also conflicts with production reality (~250 bars/batch, single boiler).

### D2 · Name: Texwax (2026-07-17)
**Decision:** The company and brand are **Texwax**. "Patina Wax" is retired.
**Reasoning:** "Patina Wax" was never a name — it was a placeholder for a heritage/living-surface thesis the founder has since rejected as the lead story ("the heritage-loving people... aren't who I should be targeting"). It's also descriptive/generic (the term exists in furniture and leather care — trademark and search problems). Texwax is neutral and ownable, and carries no baggage from the abandoned direction.
**Retained:** Patina as a *depth layer* — the bonus customers discover over months. Never the acquisition hook.

### D3 · Wedge: transformation, not maintenance; accessories-first (2026-07-17)
**Decision:** Texwax positions wax as a medium for **transforming** surfaces, not maintaining them. Launch focus is things you don't wash: **boots first**, then shoes, hats, bags.
**Reasoning:** The founder's year of living with the product revealed the category's core tension: waxed *clothing* hits a maintenance wall (wash-hostile, rewax-hungry, forgettable for anyone with a full closet) — the founder abandoned his own waxed garments. Accessories dodge the wall entirely. Boots carry a unique wedge: they can't be dyed well (soles, mixed materials, uneven takeup), but wax recolors them — including soles — and the uneven first coat reads as an attractive weathered look. Every incumbent (Otter Wax, Barbour, Filson) sells wax as care for goods you already baby; nobody owns "transform."
**Rejected:** garment-maintenance model; heritage audience as primary target.

### D4 · Target customer: the impulse transformer (2026-07-17)
**Decision:** Primary target is the fashion/shoe consumer who sees a before/after reel, thinks recoloring their boots would be fun, and buys on impulse at $25.
**Anti-customer:** the heritage maintainer who lovingly rewaxes one denim jacket. Founder's words: "few and far between."
**Known risk (accepted, managed):** impulse buyers are the highest-disappointment segment — low intent plus an expectation gap between hero content (brushed/melted, expert hands) and their likely first attempt (rub-on). Managed via the content honesty rule (D9) and the quick-start guide teaching both methods.

### D5 · Price: $25 per bar (2026-07-17)
**Decision:** $25 — deliberately in impulse territory.
**Reasoning:** Founder instinct: "low enough that people would feel comfortable impulse buying."
**Flag attached:** at $25 AOV, paid ads are likely underwater (see texwaxCommercial). This price works for the organic-content model; a bundle is needed before paid scales.

### D6 · Format: bar only (2026-07-17)
**Decision:** The product is a bar of wax, shipped in a custom padded zip envelope with a couple of stickers and a printout carrying the invoice + quick-start guide. No kits, no application tools, no pre-waxed goods, no waxing service at launch.
**Reasoning:** Founder choice, made with eyes open after the alternatives (kit / pre-transformed goods / service) were laid out as ways to close the results gap. Consequence accepted: the quick-start materials carry full responsibility for closing that gap (see D9).

### D7 · Inventory model: anchors + drops (2026-07-17)
**Decision:** Two always-on **anchor** colors at 250 bars each, plus rotating limited **drops** of ~20 bars, each drop made specifically to generate a video or two.
**Reframe adopted:** drops are *public A/B tests* — the video is the real product of a drop; the bars are almost a byproduct.
**Graduation rule:** if a drop's reel hits and the batch sells out fast, that formula gets promoted to a 250-bar anchor run. "Sold out — restocking as a core color" is a good story, not a failure; any formula can be re-made on demand.
**Open within this decision:** which two colors are the launch anchors (see OPEN-1).

### D8 · Acquisition: Instagram, organic + paid (2026-07-17)
**Decision:** Customer acquisition runs through Instagram: organic short-form reels discovered via the reels tab, plus paid promotion of marketing creative. Founder produces content personally (strong cinematography/editing/sound skills, pro camera/lenses/lights).
**Gate:** paid spend waits until order economics survive CAC (see texwaxCommercial + OPEN-2/3).

### D9 · Content: cinematic brush-method hero + honesty rule (2026-07-17)
**Decision:** Hero reels are cinematic, staged, vibey transformations using the brush/melt method — not rub-on demos. Founder's call: a rub-on reel "would be so boring," and cinematography is the whole organic advantage.
**Honesty rule (the condition attached):** the *method* must be visible in the content — melting, brushing, heat — so viewers learn what produces the result before they buy. And the funnel (quick-start card + linked tutorial via QR) must teach both a no-equipment rub/palm path and the brush path, with an explicit "the easy version works too" message. The rub-on look is legitimately good, just different (founder confirmed) — say so.
**Why the rule exists:** two decisions are in tension — brush-method hero (D9) + bar-only format (D6). The buyer may own no pot, brush, or heat gun. Content that shows magic while hiding method manufactures disappointment and one-star reviews.

### D10 · Repeat engine: the range, not rewaxing (2026-07-17)
**Decision:** Repeat revenue comes from the collection — more colors, more finishes, more items to transform — not from maintenance rewaxing.
**Reasoning:** The rewax-as-recurring-revenue thesis was tested against the founder's lived experience and killed: people don't dutifully rewax, they forget (founder's own waxed garments hang unworn). Durability numbers support this: one good boot coat lasts ~6 months at 5×/week wear, ~2 years for a light-rotation fashion consumer. This makes the effectively-unlimited formula range not just differentiation but the reason a happy customer comes back.

## OPEN — decisions still to make (rough priority order)

1. **The two launch anchor colors.** They're the always-on storefront — most first-time buyers will receive an anchor because drops sell out. Must be chosen deliberately, not defaulted. (Operator instinct on record, unconfirmed: one anchor is a black/near-black for boots.)
2. **Bundle offer.** Something like "3 for $60" — the lever that makes paid ads survivable. Decide structure and price before any paid spend.
3. **Real unit economics.** Actual COGS per bar, packaging cost, shipping cost, payment fees → contribution margin → max tolerable CAC. Currently running on the working assumption COGS < 50% of price (see Deferred).
4. **Brand foundation.** Mission, core belief, the "why" — deliberately not faked yet. Candidate raw material exists (see texwaxStrategy + texwaxIdentity).
5. **Naming system** for anchors + an endless stream of drops. Needs a scheme that scales.
6. **Visual identity & packaging design.** Logo, palette, the mailer/stickers/card, the look of the feed. Content cannot launch without this.
7. **Voice & tone.** Heritage register rejected; replacement undefined.
8. **Target customer sharpening.** Turn "impulse fashion/shoe buyer" into a real profile: age, style tribes, adjacent communities, where they are on Instagram.
9. **Competitive research.** Actual landscape mapping (see texwaxStrategy stub).
10. **Market geography.** US-first? Asked, never answered.
11. **Launch sequence.** Concrete pre-launch checklist and launch plan (texwaxRoadmap has the skeleton).
12. **Founder constraints on record.** Runway in months and hours/week were never quantified. Worth pinning down for planning.
