# Texwax Commercial

Covers: pricing · unit economics · production · the drop model · fulfillment.

---

## 1. Pricing — `SET` with open bundle

- **$25 per bar**, deliberately impulse-tier. Founder's logic: low enough that a reel viewer buys for fun without deliberation.
- **Bundle — OPEN-2, required before paid ads.** Something like "3 for $60" (structure/price undecided). The bundle exists for two reasons: it lifts average order value into territory where paid acquisition can pay for itself, and it monetizes the actual repeat driver (collecting the range). Multi-color purchase is on-thesis: different boots, different items, different looks.

## 2. Unit economics — `OPEN`, with flags on record

**Working assumption (founder, consciously deferred):** operating cost per bar < 50% of sale price. Real math not yet done.

**Operator flags already on record:**
- For a hand-made premium good, "<50% COGS" is a *low* bar. Premium brands typically need COGS nearer **20–35%** of price to fund marketing, content, and any future wholesale.
- **The $25 margin trap:** $25, minus wax + padded mailer + stickers + printout, minus US shipping (~$5–8 padded mailer), minus payment fees ≈ **single-digit dollars of contribution before any acquisition cost**. Free organic reach can carry that; **paid ads at $25 AOV usually cannot pay for themselves.**
- Rule of operation until fixed: **single-bar-at-$25 is a content model, not a paid-ads model.** Organic can run now; paid waits for the bundle and real numbers.

**To build (OPEN-3):** actual COGS per bar (materials by formula class), packaging cost, shipping cost by zone, fee load → contribution margin per order (single vs bundle) → maximum tolerable CAC → paid go/no-go threshold.

## 3. Production — `SET` facts

- One industrial boiler. **~250 bars per batch; one afternoon per batch.** Solo founder.
- Drops are ~20-bar batches — trivial to produce; the constraint on drops is content production, not manufacturing.
- Any formula can be re-made on demand → restocks and graduations are fast; "sold out" is never a long outage.
- Capacity ceiling is real but distant: at one batch/afternoon, supply outpaces any plausible early demand. Revisit only if volume forces it. (This ceiling is also part of why B2B supply was rejected at launch — see D1.)

## 4. The drop model — `SET`

- **Anchors:** 2 colors, 250 bars each, always in stock. The storefront — most first-time buyers receive an anchor. Choose them deliberately (OPEN-1).
- **Drops:** rotating ~20-bar limited formulas. Each drop exists to generate a video or two. Reframe adopted: **a drop is a public A/B test — the video is the product; the bars are almost a byproduct.** Cheap, fast taste-testing in public.
- **Graduation rule:** drop reel hits + batch sells out fast → formula is promoted to a 250-bar anchor run. Narrative: "sold out — restocking as a core color." Sellouts are proof + hype, not failure.
- Cadence: tied to the content calendar (see texwaxMarket), not to a manufacturing schedule.

## 5. Fulfillment — `PARTIAL`

- Package: custom **padded zip envelope**, a couple of **stickers**, one **printout** = invoice + quick-start guide (with QR to tutorial). Bar only — no tools, no kit (D6).
- Packaging is a brand surface — design it under visual identity (OPEN-6), don't default it.
- Undecided: shipping carrier/rates, international (market geography is OPEN-10), returns policy, e-commerce platform (lives in texwaxMarket funnel work).
